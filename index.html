The Project

Photographs that tell
a story of becoming

She Is Rising from the North: Education Works is the culmination of photographer and documentary filmmaker Hildah Kathure's fifteen years of work with girls and young women in Northern Kenya through the MOV Foundation.

"At that moment, the photograph becomes more than a record of where she has been. It becomes evidence of where she might go."

More than a collection of photographs about education, this project is a visual record of becoming — tracing girls from the landscapes where their journeys begin to the spaces they eventually occupy as professionals. It documents what happens when education opens new possibilities.

The book is divided into three sections: On the Grazing Fields, At School and On the Job — spanning from pastoralist communities in Isiolo County to boardrooms, hospitals and universities across Kenya.

The Book

Three Acts of
a Woman's Journey

01

Part One

On the Grazing Fields

The grazing fields are not simply scenery. They represent the realities many girls navigate: caring for siblings, fetching water, tending livestock and managing household responsibilities while confronting poverty, drought, insecurity and social expectations.

Explore Chapter 01
02

Part Two

At School

At school, the narrative changes. Girls begin to encounter new ideas, ask questions and imagine lives beyond their immediate surroundings. A uniform is found, fees are paid and a girl leaves home for boarding school — encountering a world of teachers, friends, knowledge and possibility.

Explore Chapter 02

Part Three

On the Job

The book introduces women such as Dr Zeinab Guracha, CEO of Kenyatta University Teaching and Referral Hospital, and Kamila Abkanu, a veterinary doctor — their journeys demonstrating that the distance between grazing fields and the workplace is social, economic and psychological.

The Filmmaker

Hildah Kathure

Photographer · Documentary Filmmaker · Advocate

Hildah Kathure is a photographer and documentary filmmaker whose work centres on education, gender and the everyday lives of communities in Northern Kenya. She has spent over fifteen years building visual archives of girls' journeys — from the moment a door opens to the moment a girl walks through it and never looks back.

"I was not simply photographing girls, but transformation. A photograph freezes a moment, but its strongest moments belong to the photographs and personal stories."

Working through the MOV Foundation, Kathure has documented close to 500 girls, from graduation photographs and certificates, to the moment that matters most — when a girl begins to see herself differently.
