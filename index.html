The Preface • Hildah Kathure

Photographs that tell
a story of becoming

We still hear the heartbreaking stories from Northern Kenya. Stories of girls who cannot go to school, or are not allowed to. Because there is no money. Because boys are still seen as more deserving of education. Because girls are married off too young, sometimes as early as eleven. Because they are needed at home — to fetch water, care for children, work the land, or look after livestock.

“Their faces remind us that inequality is not fate. Their journeys prove that education can turn a life around, not only for one girl, but for her family, her community, and the generations that follow.”

Over the last fifteen years, we have also witnessed beautiful and powerful stories of change. Girls who went to high school. Girls who continued to college, vocational training, and university. Girls who slowly discovered their voice, their confidence, and their worth. Girls who became teachers, nurses, doctors, entrepreneurs, professionals, mothers, leaders, and role models.

The photographs in this book carry those stories. They show girls and young women rising from the North — not because life was easy, but because someone believed in them, walked with them, and opened a door.

Northern Kenya is often described as marginalized. But these girls are not a margin. They are the centre of a future we all share. So we invite you to look closely at their faces, listen to their stories, and stand with us. Because change begins when we stop admiring the problem and start walking with the girls who are ready to rise.

— Hildah Kathure Photographer, Documentary Filmmaker, MOV Liaison Officer

The Book

Three Acts of
a Woman's Journey

Tracing the difficult, strenuous, and transformative journey from pastoralist grazing fields to classrooms, graduation halls, and professional life.

01

Act One

On the Grazing Fields

For many girls in Isiolo County and across Northern Kenya, education ends too early. Secondary schools are far from home. Transport is expensive or unavailable. Boarding school fees are beyond what families can afford. So the dream of school is quietly replaced by other duties — fetching water, caring for siblings, or herding animals across dry grazing fields.

In pastoralist communities, livestock is life. Yet behind every statistic is a girl: a girl walking behind goats under the hot sun, a girl carrying water before dawn, a girl watching her brother leave for school while she remains at home. This chapter begins on the grazing fields — not because this is where dreams end, but because this is where they begin.

Where Dreams Begin
02

Act Two

At School

For the girls who get the chance to continue with education, school is more than a place of learning — it is a doorway into a completely different world. Arriving at boarding school with a small bag and many uncertainties, her day begins with a bell instead of herding chores.

Slowly, she begins to change. The girl who once moved silently behind the animals begins to raise her hand in class. The girl who once doubted her voice begins to speak. She discovers science, mathematics, geography, and literature. At school, dreams become names, professions, certificates, uniforms, and new possibilities.

The Door Opens
03

Act Three

On the Job

When a girl from Northern Kenya completes her education and begins to work, her success is never hers alone. Behind her are parents who sold livestock, teachers who pushed her to keep going, and sponsors who opened a door.

She becomes a doctor, teacher, veterinary doctor, nurse, scientist, police officer, or leader. In African families, her salary becomes a lifeline paying school fees for younger siblings and feeding families. On the job, she is not just earning a living — she is carrying a story, a family, and widening the path for every girl coming behind her.

Press Release for Immediate Publication • Pictures Annexed

‘She Is Rising From The North’ – New Book and Photo Exhibition on the Transformative and Economic Power of Education

Heartbreaking stories from Northern Kenya are still abundantly around. After primary school, girls face numerous hurdles to continue their studies and fulfil their dreams. Poverty, harmful traditions, climate change and a fragile economy represent formidable barriers for girls to go to high school, let alone to pursue professional education at vocational or university level. Good news: Change is possible.

Hurdles Still Abundantly Around

Says author and photographer Hildah Kathure: ‘Travelling through Northern Kenya over many years, I have carried many painful images with me. Girls’ dreams and hopes just fade away or are replaced by domestic duties, great talents remain untapped, economic opportunities are wasted. Poverty, harmful practices, policy gaps and unequal access to educational opportunities remain hurdles to a more prosperous future.’

The Incredible Power of Education

Yet, change is possible – as exemplified by Kathure’s insightful book and photo exhibition. As MOV Liaison officer, a foundation from The Netherlands focused on education and SME support, Hildah has over the years guided hundreds of girls from poor communities to high school, while close to a hundred girls pursued education at college or university level.

The photo book depicts the often difficult and strenuous journey from the young girls on the grazing fields of Isiolo County, the teenagers at high school to the young women pursuing university studies throughout Kenya. The photographs tell life-changing stories. Of girls who discovered their voice, their confidence, their worth. Girls who became teachers, nurses, doctors, scientists, dentists, CEOs, police officers, SME entrepreneurs, governmental policy professionals, mothers, leaders, and role models. They lead, provide, decide and inspire.

Strong Economic Driver for the Long Term

Kathure: ‘Inequality is not fate. These girls’ journeys prove that education can turn a life around, for a girl, for a family, for communities, the generations that follow. Education is thus a powerful economic driver for the longer term and strategically vital to combat poverty – and I think system reform is required to take away as many hurdles as possible and open up a brave new world of opportunity.’

‘I have seen improvements over the years, such as a broader curriculum and the positive impact of digitization. Yet, the cost burden is almost impossible to carry for so many. Also the loan structure needs reform. It’s a no-brainer: Education is a main driver of prosperity, economic growth and positive development in so many respects for so many people. So why not sharpen our focus to fulfill the much-felt need for affordable education, for a fairer student financing system and sustained investment in girls from marginalized communities? Next to the pictures and life-changing stories, the book also contains various facts and figures, on disastrous poverty rates, the persistent disadvantages girls are facing and for example labor market inequality, underscoring that fundamental change is of the essence. Which will pay off, to which this book clearly testifies.’

The Author & Filmmaker

Hildah Kathure

Photographer • Documentary Filmmaker • MOV Liaison Officer • Kalmax Productions

Hildah Kathure is a photographer, documentary filmmaker, and MOV Liaison Officer whose work centres on education, gender, and the everyday lives of communities in Northern Kenya. Through Kalmax Productions and the MOV Foundation (The Netherlands), she has spent over fifteen years building visual archives of girls’ educational journeys — guiding hundreds of girls from impoverished communities to high school and onward to colleges and universities.

“I was not simply photographing girls, but transformation. A photograph freezes a moment, but its strongest truth belongs to the personal stories of resilience and the lives that keep moving forward.”

The MOV Foundation has been active in Northern Kenya for over 25 years, so far supporting some 420 girls to go to high school, of which about 100 advanced to university or college level.
